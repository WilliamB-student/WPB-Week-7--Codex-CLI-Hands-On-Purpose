Codex Reflection
William Bartemes
	Working through this, I encountered a lot of situations I had not before. I found that Codex has trouble connecting between prompts and when given more open ended requests. Not necessarily because it can't handle doing something but because without explicit guidance it can have a hard time knowing when to stop and might generate analysis that isn't particularly relevant or useful. I think this might be more of a focus for me going forward, trying to trim unnecessary things Codex creates. I imagine it's easy on a big project for these things to spiral out of control and end up with an incredibly unwieldy repository.
	Another thing that surprised me was how much time I spent trying to think of a proper prompt. It was remarkably different from how I engage with AI generally, asking questions in google search and such. In those situations, what the AI actually says isn't that important because I'm mostly looking for the sources the AI cites to but here what the AI says is very important because it is the actual material the project is made out of. Not to mention documenting each prompt is painful especially when you want to try variations of a prompt but, for me, documenting, annotating, and commenting is always one of the hardest things to motivate myself to do.
	If I had to go back, I think I'd want to sketch out the project beforehand. I, more or less, dove into it without a complete understanding or plan. I think that probably comes across in the assignment. But obviously, I wouldn't have been able to plan out trying different models. That arose organically from just seeing the shape of the data. I'm sure there's a lot of danger in that too, indulging in what interests you instead of focusing on your projects goals. This particular project seemed a lot more freeform, so it wasn't completely out of the question but in a larger project, I think those detours could end up costing a lot of time. As in most things, consideration and balance are important.
